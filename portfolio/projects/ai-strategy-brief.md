# Governed Speed: AI Governance Strategy for Sentinel Cyber Defense Branch

**Frameworks used:** NIST AI RMF (AI 100-1), NIST Generative AI Profile (AI 600-1), Risk Management Framework (RMF), ADKAR change model

> *Modeled scenario for academic use.* Sentinel Cyber Defense Branch (SCDB) is a fictional organization informed by my military, DIA and cybersecurity leadership experience. It doesn't describe a real unit or former employer. All figures are planning assumptions, not pilot results.

## 1. The problem
SCDB is a 210-person cybersecurity organization serving 14,000 users across eleven sites, with 38 systems under the Risk Management Framework. Staff already have access to enterprise generative AI, and informal use is growing faster than oversight. The branch has no AI inventory, no approval route and no evaluation standard. That means a fluent but false AI-generated claim could reach an Authorizing Official or a commander.

## 2. My approach
I recommended adopting AI *with* governance instead of around it, through a risk-tiered portfolio sequenced by how early errors can be caught:

- **Readiness assessment.** I rated Data, People, Technology and Culture, with a named owner validating each by Day 30. Data (1/5) was the main constraint.
- **Opportunity scoring.** I scored five candidate uses on value (40%), feasibility (30%) and risk manageability (30%). An override rule defers any use with irreversible or rights-affecting harm, so autonomous firewall blocking and insider-threat personnel scoring were deferred regardless of score.
- **Three-phase roadmap with evidence gates:**
  - **Quick win (Days 0–90):** AI-drafted control implementation statements for RMF packages. A security manager verifies and signs every one.
  - **Medium-term (Months 6–18):** an evidence-grounded cyber incident executive brief, released only after commander verification.
  - **Long-term (Month 18+):** a mission-impact cyber risk picture for commanders.
- **Governance structure.** An AI Review Board, risk tiers with approval routes, decision rights, six responsible-AI dimensions and a suspension-and-restart protocol that lets any user raise a stop.

## 3. What I produced
- [AI Strategy Brief, Version 3.2 (PDF)](../documents/Sibley-AI-Strategy-Brief.pdf): executive summary, readiness assessment, opportunity map, adoption roadmap, risk and governance controls, a twelve-month scorecard and leadership commitments, plus appendices on risk tiers, decision rights, test protocols and an ADKAR adoption plan.

## 4. Result / impact
- **About 1,920 hours a year freed** from 4,800 addressable hours of RMF evidence writing, worth an estimated $173,000 in labor (a modeled assumption).
- **Zero tolerance** for unsupported claims in anything released to an Authorizing Official or commander.
- **Three evidence gates** control every expansion. For example, Gate 1 tests 50 held-out statements against assessor-validated evidence before any scaling.
- **Clear use boundaries:** no autonomous action on production networks, no AI scoring of people for clearance decisions, and release only after named human verification.

## 5. What this shows about me
This is the work of an AI Director of Governance: turning AI into measurable mission value while proving, at every step, that accountable humans stay in control. It draws on my experience leading cyber intelligence at DIA and managing high-stakes portfolios, where a wrong claim reaching a decision-maker carries real consequences.
